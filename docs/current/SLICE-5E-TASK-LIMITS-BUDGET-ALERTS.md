# Development slice 5e — task time and attempt limits, and AI budget alerts

30 September 2026 (IST). Stacked on the integrated round-2 build (slices up to 4c). Synthetic data only; formal acceptance unchanged. No migration.

## What changed

- **Task limits in the capability manifest.** A capability package can now carry a task time limit (1–240 minutes, `taskTimeLimitMinutes`) and an attempt limit (1–5 attempts per task on a visit, `taskAttemptLimit`). Both are optional fields of the manifest, so they are set by the governance manager who writes the revision, included in its manifest hash, evaluated, independently reviewed and activated by an owner with the rest of the revision. Changing a limit means a new revision. Manifests written before this slice have no limits and keep their hashes. The Clinical governance form offers both fields (10 minutes and 2 attempts by default) and each capability package shows its limits.
- **Recorded on each request.** An assistant request records the limits of the revision it was prepared under (`limits`).
- **Time limit.** An unsent request now expires at its time limit, never later than the existing 15 minutes. A request sent to a provider that has no confirmed result once its time limit has passed is shown as **timed out, outcome uncertain**. It is never resent or retried. It stays open until a person records a decision with `closeTimedOutAgent` (a reason of 10–500 characters): the requesting worker, or a clinician or supervisor with normal care scope for the patient. The request is then closed as failed with `outcome: 'uncertain'`, the decision and reason; its reserved budget stays counted, and a result that arrives afterwards is withheld because the execution only accepts results for a request that is still running. The decision is audited and is allowed during maintenance. A request that has not reached its limit, or is not a sent request, is refused (409).
- **Attempt limit.** Requests for the same task on the same visit, under any revision of the same capability, count as attempts when they reached a provider (including uncertain ones) or are prepared and unexpired. Once the limit is reached, a further request is refused (429) before it is prepared and again before it is sent; the message tells the worker to continue with structured capture and that no provider substitution was made. Unsent requests that were stopped or expired free their attempt; sent ones never do. Requests without a registered capability (the unregistered local rules assistant) have no attempt limit.
- **Budget alerts.** The slice 6g operational alerts gain two rules (alert rules 1.1.0), computed from the slice 5a budget estimates and shown only to owners and administrators, who already see budgets: *AI capabilities that reached their monthly budget* (estimated use at 100% of the budget, or the next request's reservation would exceed it, so new requests are refused) and *AI capabilities at 80% or more of their monthly budget*. The count is the number of capabilities; the action names each one with its estimated use and budget. Both titles say they are estimates, not invoices. They are owned by a new owner role, **owners**: only an organisation owner (who sets budgets) can acknowledge them, following slice 6h (shared across the organisation, lasting while the count is unchanged and at most 24 hours). Administrators see them but cannot acknowledge them.

## Evidence

- 39 contract assertions (`tests/task-limits-contract.mjs`): manifests without limits parse unchanged; limit bounds; limits recorded on a prepared request, which expires at the time limit; the attempt limit refuses a third request with nothing recorded, counts tasks and visits separately, excludes the request being sent, and frees attempts only for unsent requests; timed-out detection; the timed-out decision refused for another ASHA, a supervisor outside the team, an administrator, a short reason and a request within its limit; a supervisor's decision closes it as uncertain with no further call and an audit line; a second decision refused; the uncertain request still counts as an attempt; the requester can close their own; allowed during maintenance; near and reached budget alerts from rows and from recorded estimates (including the reached state when the next reservation would exceed the budget); nothing below 80% or without budget data; owner-only acknowledgement shared with other operators.
- 1 more HTTP/D1 assertion (`tests/ai-budget-smoke.mjs`): the timed-out decision on a closed request is refused. `tests/operations-smoke.mjs` and `tests/alerts-contract.mjs` now expect alert rules 1.1.0.
- The harnesses that transpile `agent.ts` (`agent`, `agent-quality`, `ai-budget`, `capability-lifecycle`, `governance` and `provider-sandbox` contracts) now also list `task-limits`; their counts are unchanged.
- All suites: 2,297 HTTP/D1, 1,408 contract/checkpoint and 21 restore assertions. `tests/evidence/slice-5e/`.

## Limits

- Time limits are checked when the request is read or a decision is recorded; nothing runs in the background, so a timed-out request is seen when someone opens the visit. There is no notification and no queue of timed-out requests outside the visit.
- The provider call itself keeps its existing 20-second transport timeout; the task time limit governs how long an unconfirmed request may stay open before a person must decide, not how long the provider may run.
- There is no looping-provider stop that reports completed, cancelled and unresolved actions across a multi-step task: each request is still a single call.
- Budget alerts are estimates from recorded cost assumptions and reservations, not invoices; there is no invoice reconciliation, no per-provider or per-deployment budget, and nothing is delivered (no email, message or page). BR-AG-07, BR-AI-12 and BR-OPS-04 stay partial.
- HTTP tests cannot create a sent request that outlives its limit (live providers are disabled and the local provider answers at once), so the timed-out path is covered by the contract suite.
