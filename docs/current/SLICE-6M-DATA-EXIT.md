# Development slice 6m — organisation data exit

30 September 2026 (IST). Builds on the integrated round-one stack (slices 3g–6l). Synthetic data only; formal acceptance unchanged.

## What changed

- **Export.** `GET /api/data-exit` (owners only) returns one uncompressed POSIX tar archive of the organisation's stored records: `tables/<table>.jsonl` for every organisation table (28 tables plus the members' accounts), one JSON object per stored row with the stored column names; `media/<id>.<ext>` with photo and voice-note content; `handover/open-items.jsonl`; optionally `fhir/<visit>.json`; `manifest.json`; `README.txt`; and `SHA256SUMS`. `?fhir=1` adds a FHIR R4 document Bundle for each current confirmed visit, authored by the member who confirmed it. The archive is built in memory and refused with a 413 above 250,000 rows or 60 MB rather than truncated.
- **Manifest.** Format `saha-organisation-export-v1`, export identifier, generation time, environment, exporter (user id, name, account code, role, owner flag), schema version (last migration `0013_audit_chain`, workspace schema 1, row encoding), patient scope with exported and withheld counts and whether the export is complete, totals, and for every file its path, rows, withheld rows, bytes, SHA-256, stored columns and omitted columns. Files are listed in an order that satisfies foreign keys, so rows can be re-inserted in that order. `SHA256SUMS` covers every other file for `sha256sum -c`.
- **Scope.** Owner authority does not override patient scope. Patient-linked rows (patients, visits, plans, AI results, submissions, referrals, revisions, media, rights requests, identity cases, command receipts) are exported only for patients in the owner's normal care scope: team membership, ASHAs their assigned patients, administrators none. Emergency access grants are not export authority. Households and caseload transfers follow team scope. Other rows are counted as withheld. Audit events outside scope stay in the chain as their sequence, hashes and content digest only, so the chain still verifies.
- **Left out.** Invitation token hashes, command receipt results (a `createInvite` receipt carries the new invitation token), account email and active-organisation pointers, and the Release 0.1 per-account `workspaces` source (its migrated content is in the organisation tables and `legacy_migrations` holds its hash). Credential bindings are names of server secrets, not values; no secret is stored in the database. The manifest lists every exclusion with its reason.
- **Hand-over list.** Unresolved external actions (programme submissions queued, sending, uncertain or retryable; EHR operations running or uncertain) with "look up the receipt before any retry; never resend blindly", and open patient tasks (open follow-ups and non-terminal referrals) with a named owner and that owner's membership status (`active`, `suspended`, `not-a-member`, `unassigned`). The manifest counts tasks without an active owner.
- **Consistent chain end** (follow-up, after slice 2d). The chain is verified and its head read before any table; audit events chained after that head are left out, and the manifest records it as `auditChain.exportedHead`, which the verifier checks the archive's last chained event against. An export taken while commands run therefore still verifies.
- **Audit.** Each export is recorded in the audit chain as `organisationExport`, with the export identifier, counts, the chain status at export, and the manifest and archive SHA-256, which are also returned in `X-Saha-Manifest-SHA256` and `X-Saha-Content-SHA256`. The chain is verified immediately before the export and the result, including the head, is in the manifest. Maintenance does not block the export: the existing rule pauses commands, not reads.
- **Offline verifier.** `node scripts/verify-export.mjs <archive.tar | directory>` needs only Node. It checks the format, `SHA256SUMS`, every manifest entry's size, SHA-256 and row count, the totals, that every row belongs to the organisation, 20 kinds of reference between tables, media content against the row's base64 SHA-256 and size, FHIR documents against confirmed visits, the audit chain from sequence 0 to the head recorded in the manifest, and, when no earlier event is withheld, the genesis seal over events written before the chain. It prints a JSON report and exits 1 on any failure.
- **Screen.** Organisation & team gains a "Data export for exit or transfer" panel for owners: a FHIR option, a download button, and the export identifier and checksum prefixes after download.

## Evidence

- 39 contract checks (37 in the slice, 2 in the follow-up; `tests/data-exit-contract.mjs`, real migrations in SQLite through the D1 shim): archive layout; `tar -tf` lists it and `sha256sum -c SHA256SUMS` passes after `tar -xf`; manifest identification and the schema version tied to the last migration file; every file's SHA-256, bytes and rows; a north-team patient withheld from a default-team owner, with the name absent from the whole archive and the patient's audit event present as a digest; invitation token hash, receipt token and account email absent; media content matching its row; one FHIR document authored by the confirming member; the hand-over list with an uncertain submission and tasks owned, not owned by a member, and completed (excluded); the verifier passes on a real archive and extracted directory; a changed byte, a resealed audit edit (reported at its sequence), a resealed dropped patient (dangling visit reference), a foreign row, changed media and a truncated archive are each detected; building writes nothing; an administrator-role owner gets configuration and digests only and the chain still verifies; 1,203 events from before the chain reproduce the genesis seal and a resealed change to one of them is detected; a 1000-person organisation (1,000 patients and visits, 100 referrals) exports completely, verifies, and re-imports into a fresh migrated database with equal row counts in all 28 tables, identical loaded patients and visits and an intact chain at the same head; an over-limit export is refused; a command committed while the export reads its tables is chained after the recorded head and left out, the archive still verifies, and a manifest claiming a later head than the archive holds is caught.
- 31 HTTP/D1 assertions (`tests/data-exit-smoke.mjs`): ASHA, clinician, supervisor, administrator and non-member refused; bad FHIR flag and format refused; the owner's export of the shared test organisation has the headers and checksums, the manifest matches the database (patients exported plus withheld, every patient the owner sees, one FHIR document per current confirmed visit), secrets and emails other than stored display names are absent, the export event carries both checksums and follows the exported chain, and a changed byte fails verification. The verifier, run as a receiver would with `node scripts/verify-export.mjs`, passes every structural check; on this organisation it fails only the genesis seal, as the server does, because the retention suite inserts an old event without chain fields after the chain started. A new organisation paused for maintenance still exports, completely, and verifies with every check passing.
- Browser check (headless Chromium, built Worker, synthetic owner): the panel downloaded a `.tar` with FHIR requested, the confirmation named the archive checksum prefix, the verifier passed all ten checks on the downloaded file, axe-core found no violations in the panel, no console errors. `tests/evidence/slice-6m/browser-data-exit.json`.
- All suites: see [VALIDATION.md](../../VALIDATION.md). Reports in `tests/evidence/slice-6m/`.

## Limits

- An owner's export is complete only when the owner's care teams cover every patient. There is no buyer-level export authority that crosses care scope, and no signed agreement flow for exit.
- The archive is neither encrypted nor signed. Its checksums show it is unchanged since export; they do not show who produced it. A forger with the archive alone can rewrite rows and reseal the checksums; the audit chain catches changes to audit events, and the recorded archive and manifest checksums catch everything else only when compared with the audit event.
- Where the sign-in service provides no name, the application stores the email as the display name, and it appears in the audit trail and records as stored.
- The whole archive is built in one request, bounded at 250,000 rows and 60 MB. Larger organisations need staged exports.
- Tables are read page by page, not in one transaction. Only the audit chain is cut at a recorded point; a record changed during the export may appear in its newer state, and a new record may appear without its audit event.
- Re-import is shown only inside the contract suite. There is no import tool, no third-party re-import and no buyer sign-off of the format. JSON columns are exported as stored; field-level units and provenance are documented only where the application already records them.
- The hand-over list names owners; it does not transfer tasks, cancel queues or record a provider-termination disposition. BR-OPS-07 and NFR-22 stay partial.
