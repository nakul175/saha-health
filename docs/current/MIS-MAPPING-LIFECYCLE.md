# B07 — reviewed mapping lifecycle

5 October 2026. Local synthetic software evidence; native destination/terminology and clinical acceptance remain pending. Based on canonical `1d388bd2`, with the dispatch ledger read from `11761a9e`.

The prior built Worker rejected an effective-dated mapping draft with HTTP 422. `configureMis` previously replaced its mapping immediately, suspended the connection and changed the connector version. It had no reviewed source manifest, effective time, dry-run migration impact or rollback revision. The actual before-gap observation is in `tests/evidence/mapping-lifecycle/before-gap.json`.

Owners now have a reachable **Connections → Programme MIS → Reviewed mapping revisions** panel in both the app and browser. They can create a source-versioned immutable draft, calculate its current impact, review that exact impact, publish once its UTC effective time arrives, or cancel it while retaining evidence. A rollback creates a new draft copied from a previously applied revision or a configured legacy baseline; it follows the same impact/review/publication gates. An unconfigured empty baseline cannot be published by rollback.

## Stored contract and authority

The six strict commands are `draftMisMapping`, `impactMisMapping`, `reviewMisMapping`, `applyMisMapping`, `cancelMisMapping` and `rollbackMisMapping`. They require current organisation ownership, connector version and lifecycle version. Saved receipt reads also require current ownership; they do not repeat a mutation or reconstruct a revision. The ordinary organisation CAS, membership guards, chained audit and command receipt persist the change atomically. There is no migration: the existing integration JSON stores the lifecycle, and the existing plan/delivery JSON stores optional server-derived mapping references.

A manifest pins the selected field mapping, programme coverage, linked-correction setting, declared source reference/version, UTC effective time and optional terminology declarations. Its hash and body are never edited by subsequent commands. Lifecycle events append actor, time, impact hash/counts and publication connector version. Mutable current/pending pointers select those immutable revisions. Legacy baselines are explicitly unreviewed/untracked historical configuration snapshots captured at lifecycle adoption; their displayed timestamp is capture time, not evidence of the old mapping's actual effective date or approval.

A lifecycle management version changes when a draft/impact/review is saved; the connector version does not. Existing approval bodies therefore remain usable before a scheduled change is due. Once a reviewed pending change is due, fresh prepare/approve/execute and clinical send gates refuse until the owner refreshes impact and publishes or cancels it. Explicit metadata probes remain available under their existing rules. Receipt lookup remains a metadata-only resolution of the original route/key/hash, not permission to resend under a replacement mapping.

Publication requires a freshly matching impact hash and a matching review by a currently active owner. It replaces only current configuration, advances connector version, removes the previous probe result and suspends the connection. All old unsent approvals are stale even when their projected body is unchanged. Existing connection probing/activation remains required and server-gated. No provider is enabled by this feature. Pending managed revisions cannot be bypassed with legacy `configureMis`.

New server-prepared plans and deliveries pin `{id, hash}` of the current mapping revision in their internal metadata. Existing approval payloads, hashes, keys, correction links, terminal receipts, legacy/imported history, programme captures and pack manifests are preserved. No new field is silently added to the outbound wire envelope.

## Impact and measurement mapping

The impact command returns fixed aggregate counts only: unsent approvals, changed projected bodies, unmappable unsent approvals, pending plans/changed plans, uncertain or sending outcomes requiring lookup, retained terminal outcomes, deprecated declarations and ambiguous declarations. It returns no patient IDs, names, notes or payloads. This is owner configuration impact over the retained organisation state, not a new patient-detail entitlement or patient-specific repair queue. Its audit records the configuration read; returned snapshots retain their normal scoped/read-audit path.

The server hash binds organisation/connector, current mapping and programmes, immutable candidate hash, selected submission IDs/states/digests/receipts, current person/consent/visit versions, pending plan digests/states and pinned programme/range captures. A changed material record or approval invalidates the prepared impact; publication refuses rather than silently recomputing and applying. The owner must calculate and review the new result. No approval bodies are migrated in place: workers cancel a confirmed unsent approval and prepare a fresh plan under the published/tested version. Uncertain outcomes remain lookup-only.

Each numerical measurement has explicit opt-in `Source`, `ObservedAt` and `Unit` source selectors, e.g. `weightSource`, `systolicObservedAt`, `temperatureUnit`. They use recorded per-measurement provenance with the existing legacy visit fallback. Missing-value reasons may be mapped as source metadata, but a missing required value still refuses the payload. Units describe canonical saved values (`kg`, `mmHg`, `°C`, `weeks`, `days`), not the alternative unit originally entered. Historical entry/conversion evidence remains unchanged.

Declared `partner-local` or `LOINC` codes attach to explicit destination targets and name a fixed status (`active`, `deprecated`, `ambiguous`), with optional replacement metadata. Deprecated or ambiguous declarations appear in impact and prevent review/publication; owners cancel and create a resolved new revision. These are **owner declarations**, not a terminology registry lookup, proof of a clinically correct LOINC association or native destination approval. No code substitution or automatic semantic migration occurs.

Local bounds are 5,000 retained visits plus submissions, 50 immutable revisions, 20 mapped fields/declarations, and a 200-event threshold for new impact/review/publication work. Draft/cancel evidence is retained; reaching a bound refuses work rather than deleting history or claiming a partial impact is complete. Larger-history archival/migration and an externally approved terminology registry/corpus remain explicit follow-up boundaries. Same-time scheduling does not silently activate a provider: owner publication is an intentional checkpoint.
