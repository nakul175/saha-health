# Development slice 10g — disputes block sending, sign-off rules per programme and destination, sign-off withdrawal

2 October 2026 (UTC). Builds on slice 2g (note and answer verification and the optional supervisor sign-off), slice 7e (operation checks inside the execution checkpoint), slice 6y (task trace) and slices 6u and 7f (EHR publications closed as not sent). Synthetic data only; formal acceptance unchanged. Four commands are added (`raiseDispute`, `resolveDispute`, `withdrawSignOff` and the owner command `setSignOffRules`); no route and no migration. The first three name a visit and are certified patient commands (52 to 55 in `lib/saha/partition.ts`).

Slice 2g left three gaps in BR-DATA-02: a disputed fact could still be sent, the sign-off requirement was one switch for the whole organisation, and a sign-off could not be taken back. This slice closes the first two for programme and EHR sends and the third completely. BR-DATA-02 stays partial.

## What changed

- **One gate for every send.** `lib/saha/send-gate.ts` is a leaf module (it imports only the core helpers, fact provenance and types) that answers "may this visit go to this destination now?". The dispatcher (`ensureSignOff`, which slice 2g introduced), `executeMis`, `executeEhr` and the screens all call it, so a screen cannot disagree with the server. Its checks are pure over stored state, so the executors can repeat them on a fresh read.
- **Raising a dispute.** `raiseDispute` names a confirmed, current visit and one item on it: a recorded measurement (`fact:bloodPressure`, `fact:weight`, `fact:temperature`, `fact:weeks`, `fact:coughDays`), the notes, or one programme answer (`answer:<question id>`). It needs a reason of at least 10 characters and a reference (where the conflicting information comes from) of 3 to 200. An ASHA (for an assigned patient), clinician or supervisor in normal care scope may raise one (emergency access does not count); administrators and providers may not. The dispute stores who raised it, the visit version, a SHA-256 of the disputed content and a short text of the value, so the evidence of what was disputed survives any later edit. One open dispute per item, at most 50 per visit; the visit's own facts are not changed. Each dispute writes a record revision and an audit event and is rechecked on replay under the current membership.
- **What an open dispute stops.** While a dispute is open on a current visit, the dispatcher refuses (409, with the reason, reference, who raised it and when) `preparePlan`, `approvePlan` and `executePlan` for any programme destination, and `prepareEhrPublish` for the EHR connection, that would carry the item. A demonstration programme destination records the whole visit; an HTTP programme destination carries only the facts its mapping reads (and the notes if mapped), so a disputed blood pressure does not stop a destination that maps weight only; programme answers are not part of any mapping yet, so an answer dispute stops every programme destination (it fails closed); the EHR document carries every recorded measurement and the notes, and no programme answers; no other connection kind is ever sent a visit. The text ends "Anything already sent is not recalled."
- **Resolving a dispute.** `resolveDispute` is for a clinician in normal care scope. Evidence of at least 10 characters (and an optional reference) is appended as a resolution; the dispute, its reason and the original value stay. Outcome `stands` needs the disputed content to be unchanged, and a clinician who neither recorded the item nor raised the dispute (a clinician who raised one cannot close it as standing, and the recorder, or the confirmer where no author was recorded, cannot either). Outcome `corrected` needs the content to have changed since the dispute was raised, which happens on a correction draft. Each resolution writes a record revision and an audit event.
- **Correcting the visit does not clear a dispute.** `amendVisit` copies the still-open disputes of the corrected visit to the correction draft (new ids, `carriedFrom` the original, a `carryDisputes` audit event); the correction can be sent only after a clinician resolves them on it. A visit that was corrected or marked as wrong-patient is never sent again, so its disputes stop nothing.
- **Sign-off rules per programme and destination.** `setSignOffRules` (owner only, checked on replay as well) sets a rule of required or not required for any of the five programmes and for any programme or EHR connection, or removes it ("follow the default"). The rules share the version of the organisation default, so a stale view of either is refused (409); a change that changes nothing is refused; unknown programmes and non-programme, non-EHR connections are refused (422); up to 60 rules; each change keeps its reason in the policy history and writes a `setSignOffRules` audit event. The requirement for a send is decided in this order: a rule for the destination; otherwise the most protective rule among the programmes the destination serves that the patient is in (any programme that requires sign-off requires it; a programme with no rule falls back to the default); otherwise the organisation default, which covers programme destinations only and never covered EHR publication, so an EHR connection needs its own or a programme rule. Turning the organisation default on or off (slice 2g's `setSignOffPolicy`) now keeps the rules.
- **Withdrawing a sign-off.** `withdrawSignOff` lets a supervisor of the patient's team, or a current supervision delegate, withdraw the visit's current sign-off with a reason of at least 10 characters. The sign-off record is kept and a withdrawal is appended (who, when, why, the delegation if one was used, the sign-off it withdrew). While the latest sign-off is withdrawn, sending is blocked whatever the rules say (a withdrawal is a hold, even where the rule for that destination is "not required"), until a supervisor signs the visit off again (`signOffVisit`; a new sign-off is allowed because the old one is no longer current). A withdrawal after a send changes nothing that was sent.
- **Inside the execution checkpoint.** The checks run in the same three places slice 7e's operation checks do. Programme deliveries: `executePlan` and `sendSubmission` check disputes, the withdrawal hold and the destination's rule before the checkpoint (a refusal there leaves the item prepared or queued, as before); the state is then checkpointed; and the checks run again on a fresh read immediately before the request. A dispute raised or a sign-off withdrawn in between stops the request: the delivery is closed as not sent (`cancelled`, attempts released) with a `block` record (kind, stage `checkpoint`, time, the dispute ids), and no request is made. EHR publications: `executeEhrPublish` checks before the checkpoint, again on a fresh read before the patient is read, and again after the patient read, immediately before the document is created; a block closes the publication as `blocked` with the same `block` record. Both write a `sendBlocked` audit event and the task trace step is linked to it; the publication that relied on a sign-off records which one. The sign-off wording of slice 2g is kept where a sign-off is simply missing or stale. *Decision:* dispute and sign-off blocks are not written as `refusal` records and do not add an exception-queue kind (the queue's rules version is unchanged); a visit with an open dispute or a withdrawn sign-off is visible on the visit itself.
- **Screens.** A confirmed visit shows a Disputes panel (each dispute with its reason, reference, who and when, any resolution with its evidence, a notice that sending is blocked, a form to raise one and, for a clinician, a form to resolve one), and the sign-off panel adds the withdrawal form, the withdrawn and hold states and the note that anything already sent is not recalled. The Organisation view adds "Sign-off rules per programme and destination" for owners (one control per programme and per programme or EHR connection, a reason, and the order in which rules apply) and its policy text now says "organisation default". Every panel says that nothing already sent is recalled and that a dispute recorded in the verification review (slices 2e and 2g) is a review and does not stop sending.
- **Who sees the reason.** The dispatcher check runs before a command's own authority check, so the dispute and withdrawal wording (reason, reference, name) is shown only to a member in the patient's normal care scope; anyone else who reaches it is told only that sending is blocked for the visit and to ask the care team.

## Limits

- A dispute raised in the verification review (`verifyEntry` or `verifyFact` with decision disputed, slices 2e and 2g) is a clinician's review and still does not stop sending; only a dispute raised in the slice 10g form does. Joining the two would change every earlier test and would let a single reviewer block a small team, so the screens say which is which.
- An answer dispute stops every programme destination because no mapping carries answers; an HTTP destination is stopped for a fact only if its mapping reads it (a mapping is by source field, so a disputed blood pressure stops a destination that maps either reading).
- ABDM transfer and consent, aggregate reports and receipt lookups are not gated by disputes or sign-off. A disputed fact in a visit that an aggregate report counts is not removed from the report.
- A blocked send raises no exception-queue item and no notification; the block is shown on the visit, on the plan or publication, in the audit and on the trace. A dispute or withdrawal that happens after the request has left changes nothing, because nothing is recalled.
- A clinician who raised a dispute cannot close it as standing; in a team with one clinician another clinician from outside the team cannot resolve it either (care scope is required), so the dispute stays open until one is assigned, which blocks sending by design.
- The organisation default has never covered EHR publication; that is unchanged. A withdrawal is a hold whatever the rules say, including where the rule for the destination is "not required".
- Disputes, resolutions and withdrawals are not in FHIR or programme payloads; coded FHIR provenance is still not built.
- The HTTP smoke prepares an EHR publication but does not execute one (the connection is registered and not activated, and no live provider is used); EHR execution is covered by the contract test.
- BR-DATA-02 stays partial. Nothing here is accepted for production or real patient use.
