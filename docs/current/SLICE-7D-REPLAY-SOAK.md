# Development slice 7d — replay and timeout soak of programme and EHR delivery, and adapter overhead benchmark

2 October 2026 IST (1 October UTC). Builds on the programme delivery checkpoints (`lib/saha/mis-execution.ts`, [MIS-API.md](../../MIS-API.md)), the EHR publication checkpoints (`lib/saha/ehr-execution.ts`, [EHR-ABDM.md](../../EHR-ABDM.md)), slice 6u (EHR publications closed as not sent) and slice 6y (`linkTrace`). Synthetic data only; no provider or destination was called. Test and evidence only: no product code, route, command or migration changed, because the soak found no defect.

## What changed

- **Soak test** (`tests/replay-soak-contract.mjs`). It runs the real `executeMis` and `executeEhr`, the real HTTP adapters `misRequest` and `fhirRequest` (headers, response size limits, JSON parsing) and the real `applyReceipt` that the signed callback route uses, against a local mock partner in the same process. The mock programme partner (saha-mis-v1) randomly accepts, rejects, leaves work pending, times out before accepting, times out after accepting, drops, truncates or resets the response after accepting, returns HTTP 503, or acknowledges with a mismatched payload hash; its lookups randomly answer, time out or are dropped; pending work completes on a later lookup; terminal outcomes are sent as signed-callback receipts, one to three copies, some delivered while Saha's send is still in flight and some later, concurrently with other commands. The mock EHR (FHIR R4) randomly times out or fails the patient read, accepts the document, refuses it with 422, times out before or after storing, drops the response after storing, or acknowledges changed content; its identifier searches randomly answer, time out or are dropped. The driver adds command replays with the same key (answered from the stored command receipt, as `lib/saha/server.ts` does) and with a fresh key, two or three concurrent sends or publications of one item, concurrent lookups, and rounds in which the final receipt write is interrupted, leaving a checkpoint that a later lookup must resolve after its 30 or 45 second wait (the clock is simulated by back-dating the checkpoint).
- **A deliberately naive partner.** The mock partners do not deduplicate by idempotency key and do not honour `If-None-Exist`, so any resend by Saha after the partner had accepted would appear as a second accepted record or a second stored document. Every programme submission must carry `Idempotency-Key` equal to its body key, every lookup must carry no visit fields, and every EHR write must carry the conditional-create header.
- **What is checked.** (1) No idempotency key ever has a second accepted programme record. (2) No programme submission reaches the partner for a key whose earlier outcome had not been answered as `not_found` by a delivered lookup, and none after a terminal outcome: an uncertain outcome is resolved only by a receipt (lookup or signed callback), never by a blind resend. (3) After a final reconciliation pass (faults off, lookups only, plus sends for items a lookup had confirmed unsent), every Saha programme state matches the partner ledger: accepted with the partner's receipt and exactly one accepted record, rejected, or retryable only when all three attempts are used and the partner holds nothing; partner submissions never exceed Saha's recorded attempts. (4) No EHR identifier has a second stored document or a second write request. (5) Every Saha EHR state matches the EHR ledger: accepted with the stored document's id, closed as not sent with no write, or unresolved only where the EHR stored nothing. (6) The run met its event target and exercised every fault class. (7) Lookups were answered for both adapters.
- **Counting.** The target counts timeouts, lost responses (dropped, truncated or reset), same-key and fresh-key replays, extra concurrent attempts and lookups, and duplicate callbacks. HTTP errors, mismatched or changed acknowledgements, interrupted final writes and compare-and-swap conflicts are exercised and reported but not counted toward the target. The bounded CI variant stops at 1,500 counted events (about 3 seconds); `SAHA_SOAK_EVENTS=10000` runs the full soak (about 14 seconds). It is in the CI contract loop.
- **Benchmark** (`tests/adapter-overhead-bench.mjs`, not in CI). It measures the Saha side of each adapter: transformation (the mapped programme payload and its hash; the FHIR document build, outbound validation, permission check and hash of `prepareEhrPublish`), the send or publication command (state read, checkpoint, request and response handling, receipt or acknowledgement validation, final write) and the lookup command. The partners answer at once and the time spent inside their handlers is subtracted. Persistence is an in-memory compare-and-swap model that runs the real `rowsForState` diff from `lib/saha/store.ts` and serialises the changed rows but writes nothing to D1.

## Evidence

- Full soak, seed 7: 10,027 counted events (2,117 timeouts and lost responses, 7,910 replays and duplicates) over 416 programme organisations (1,248 submissions) and 416 EHR organisations (1,248 publications). Programme: 1,087 accepted, 150 rejected, 11 retryable with all three attempts used; 1,087 accepted partner records for 1,087 accepted submissions, no duplicate accepted record, no blind resend. EHR: 875 accepted, 106 closed as not sent, 267 unresolved; 875 stored documents, no duplicate stored document, no repeated write. Seeds 11 and 23 (10,036 and 10,031 counted events) gave the same result. Reports: `tests/evidence/slice-7d/replay-soak-10000.json`, `replay-soak-10000-seed11.json`, `replay-soak-10000-seed23.json`.
- Counted events in the seed 7 run: submission timeouts 487, programme lookup timeouts 148, EHR write timeouts 335, EHR lookup timeouts 273, EHR patient-read timeouts 51; lost programme submission responses 182, lost programme lookup responses 164, lost EHR write responses 169, lost EHR lookup responses 308; same-key replays 653, fresh-key replays 3,189, extra concurrent attempts 2,768, extra concurrent lookups 826, duplicate callbacks 474. Also exercised: 73 programme and 161 EHR HTTP errors, 83 mismatched programme receipts, 137 changed EHR acknowledgements, 355 interrupted final writes (193 programme and 344 EHR stuck checkpoints resolved by later lookup), 1,129 callbacks delivered (464 applied, 665 recognised as replays, none refused) and 9,273 compare-and-swap conflicts between concurrent commands in one organisation.
- Adapter overhead, local, 200 measured iterations per stage after 30 warm-up, on a synthetic organisation of 200 patients, 600 confirmed visits, 300 earlier programme submissions with four events each and 1,500 audit events, a six-field programme mapping and one linked EHR patient, partner handler time subtracted (milliseconds, p50 / p95 / p99): programme payload mapping and hash 5.1 / 13.1 / 23.9; programme send 81.8 / 119.4 / 166.5; programme lookup 84.2 / 124.1 / 146.0; mapping plus send 87.2 / 129.1 / 182.0; EHR document preparation and validation 5.2 / 13.5 / 53.3; EHR publication 86.8 / 158.5 / 219.6; EHR lookup 85.2 / 130.0 / 171.5; preparation plus publication 95.6 / 167.0 / 237.7. Most of the time is checkpointing (copying the whole organisation state and diffing its rows twice per command), not transformation. Measured on a 2-CPU build machine at a load average of 6 to 9 from other builders' test suites, so the figures include that contention. Report: `tests/evidence/slice-7d/adapter-overhead.json`.
- The bounded soak (1,500 counted events, 7 checks) runs in the full suite and CI. All suites: see [VALIDATION.md](../../VALIDATION.md). Reports in `tests/evidence/slice-7d/`.

## Findings

- **No defect found.** No duplicate accepted partner activity and no blind resend in about 30,000 counted events across three seeds.
- **EHR publications often stay unresolved under faults.** About a fifth of EHR publications (267 of 1,248 in the seed 7 run) ended unresolved where the EHR had stored nothing: the write was refused with 422 or timed out before storing (261), or the command was interrupted after a pre-write refusal and before its "not sent" outcome was stored (6). The EHR adapter has no definitive not-found answer: an identifier search with no match leaves the publication unresolved by design ([EHR-ABDM.md](../../EHR-ABDM.md)), so each needs partner reconciliation, and none was resent. The fault rates are deliberately high; the proportion is not a prediction. Treating a definitive empty search from a partner that enforces conditional create as "not stored" would be a product decision for a later slice, not made here.
- **Programme deliveries can exhaust their three attempts.** 11 programme submissions used all three attempts with the partner confirming no record each time; Saha refuses a fourth send as documented, and the partner holds nothing.

## Limits

- Persistence is an in-memory organisation compare-and-swap model, not D1; command receipts are modelled from that model's stored receipts. The callback path runs `applyReceipt` and the route's replay rules after authentication, not the HTTP route, its signature check or D1 (those are covered by the API suite). Time passing for stuck checkpoints is simulated.
- The mock partners' lookups are consistent and no request arrives late. A partner whose lookup is eventually consistent, or which receives an earlier request after a confirmed `not_found`, must still deduplicate, as MIS-API.md requires; that partner behaviour is not tested here.
- The run is seeded, but concurrent interleavings depend on scheduling, so two runs with the same seed can differ in detail.
- The benchmark is a local measurement on the build machine with a stated synthetic workload while other builders ran test suites on the same machine. It is not the R1 reference workload or a reference environment: D1 latency, Worker isolate limits and real partner payloads are excluded. The assistant (agent) adapter is not benchmarked.
- NFR-05 stays partial (live partner replay evidence and partner deduplication remain); NFR-28 stays unverified (no reference environment or R1 workload).

## Correction — 2 October 2026 (slice 6ab)

An eighth independent review found the soak's oracle too loose: a resend counted as allowed once the partner had delivered `not_found` even if Saha never stored it; duplicates were checked per idempotency key with pre-seeded submissions only; and the callback path was a copy without the route's event limit and endpoint/binding checks. Slice 6ab requires Saha's stored delivery events to show a recorded `not_found` before a resend, runs signed callbacks through the real route function, adds bounded organisations that create, cancel, withdraw and re-prepare submissions through the real plan commands, and checks at most one accepted record per visit. The stricter soak also passes on the code before 6ab. See [SLICE-6AB-EIGHTH-REVIEW-FIXES.md](SLICE-6AB-EIGHTH-REVIEW-FIXES.md).
