# Development slice 1f — scheduled caseload transfers that apply at their effective time, and household transfer

1–2 October 2026 (UTC). Builds on slices 1, 1b and 6v. Synthetic data only; formal acceptance unchanged.

## What changed

- **Due transfers apply without a person pressing Apply.** A scheduled caseload transfer (slice 1b) now applies automatically the first time Saha is used at or after its effective time. Every organisation load for a read or a command (`loadFor` in `lib/saha/server.ts`) first asks one indexed question (`lib/saha/caseload-schedule.ts`): does this organisation have a scheduled transfer whose effective time has passed, for a caller who is an active member? Only then is the organisation loaded in full, the due transfers are applied in effective-time order (`applyDueTransfers` in `lib/saha/caseload.ts`), and the result is committed before the request reads its own state. A steady state costs that one query and nothing else. A stranger, a removed member or a suspended member never triggers it.
- **Same re-checks as a manual application.** Each due transfer is applied with the authority of the steward who scheduled it, re-checked at that moment: the steward is still an active member who stewards the team (a supervisor or an owner with patient access in the team), the receiver is still an active ASHA or clinician in the team, and each patient is still in the steward's scope, still in the team and still held by the sending worker. A patient whose assignment changed is skipped and listed, as before. Moves are the same as slice 1b: all open follow-ups, referrals, outreach items and message follow-ups go to the receiver, field packages are revoked and unapproved sharing plans are blocked.
- **Record revision and audit per application.** Each moved patient gets a record revision with action `caseloadAutoApply` and the scheduler as actor (`system`, "Saha scheduled transfer"); the handover event on the patient names the transfer and is acknowledged by the receiver exactly as before. One audit event `caseloadAutoApply` per transfer records the application time, the effective time, the number moved and skipped (no patient names). The commit writes a command receipt for the scheduler (`caseload-auto-…`). The transfer records `applied.automatic: true`.
- **Held when a re-check fails.** If the scheduling steward is no longer active or no longer stewards the team, the receiver is no longer eligible, or no patient is still held by the sending worker, nothing moves and the transfer becomes `held` with the reason and time (`held: {at, reason}`), audited once as `caseloadHold`. A held transfer is never retried automatically; it shows as "held: apply or cancel" in the Care handover page with its reason. A steward may apply it (`caseloadApply`, with the usual re-checks under the steward's own authority) or cancel it (`caseloadCancel`). Held transfers count as pending: their patients cannot be put in another transfer and their members cannot be removed.
- **Exactly once under concurrency.** The application commit uses the organisation-wide revision guard. Several requests arriving together after the effective time may all evaluate, but only the first commit succeeds; the others are refused by the guard (409), leave the outcome to the winner, and carry on with their own read or command. Evaluation is best effort: a failure is logged without content and the request continues; the next use tries again. Maintenance pauses automatic application until it ends.
- **Household transfer.** `caseloadHouseholdTransfer` (team, sending and receiving worker, household id and version, effective time or now, reason) moves every current member of one household that the sending worker holds in the team as one transfer, immediately or scheduled, with the same acknowledgement and application rules. It is refused for non-stewards (403), for a stale household version (409), and when no member is held by the sending worker (409). A household with any current member outside the requester's care scope is refused as a whole (403, giving only the count of such members, never their names); a household with no member in the requester's scope answers as not found (404). Members in scope but held by another worker or team are reported in `notIncluded` and stay where they are. The transfer records the household; at application, a member who has left the household since scheduling is skipped with that reason. Role and scope are checked on the server and again when an idempotent request is replayed; the command carries ids only, never free text in URLs.
- **Workspace.** The caseload preview names each patient's current household. The transfer dialog offers "Transfer by: Selected patients / Whole household: …"; choosing a household selects its members held by the sending worker and sends the household command. The transfers table marks household transfers, transfers applied automatically and held transfers with their reason, and offers Apply and Cancel for held ones.
- **No migration.** Held state, the household and the automatic flag are stored in the existing `caseload_transfers` row (state column and JSON data).

## Why there is no exact-time scheduler

The Worker has no background timer in this deployment (the same constraint as slice 6v alert delivery). With nobody using Saha nothing is evaluated, so a transfer due at 06:00 applies when the first member of that organisation opens or changes anything afterwards, and its recorded application time is that moment, not the effective time. Until then the sending worker still holds the patients. A Cloudflare Cron Trigger calling `applyDueCaseloads` for each organisation with due transfers every few minutes would bound that delay independently of use; the function is already idempotent and guarded for that, but no trigger is configured here and no deployment was changed.

## Limits

- Application time depends on use, as above. No Cron Trigger, reminder or notification tells the receiver or the steward that a transfer applied or was held; they see it in the Care handover page.
- Transfer by locality, caseload balancing suggestions, field-device acceptance of a handover and approved urgency rules are not built; the two-day urgency window remains a local default.
- A household transfer takes members as they are at request time; someone who joins the household after scheduling is not added. Members held by another worker are reported, not moved.
- A held transfer keeps the steward's original reason and effective time; there is no rescheduling command (cancel and schedule again).
- Each application or hold is one organisation-wide commit; in a busy organisation it may make a concurrent command retry, as any organisation-wide command does.
