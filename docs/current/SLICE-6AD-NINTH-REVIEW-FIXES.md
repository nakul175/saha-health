# Development slice 6ad — fixes from a ninth independent review of the round-5 slices (mainly 7g aggregate delivery)

2 October 2026 (UTC). Builds on `int10-3k-notebook-capacity` (`4749241`: rounds 1–6 of the overnight build). Synthetic data only; formal acceptance unchanged. No command, route or migration is added; the route count stays 49 and the certified patient command count stays 52.

A ninth independent review, carried out by an agent that did not write the code, examined the round-5 slices 6aa, 7f, 2j and 7g. That code had been built, tested and recorded as passing; the earlier runs did not test these cases. Three findings were rated high and block merging the overnight work into `main`; they are fixed first. Each product defect was reproduced on the base before it was fixed (the reviewer's `t1.mjs` and the new regression checks); the new checks fail on the base and pass after the fix. The slice 7g and 6aa documents carry a dated correction; their original text is kept.

## What the review found, and the fix

1. **Report cells were shown to people before any check for that person (high, slice 7g).** Preparation screened a report only against the destination's releases and recorded nothing; `GET /api/aggregate-reports` returned the full payload cells to the preparer, the approver and every manager covering the teams. A supervisor could prepare reports with different periods or groupings, never send them, and see each one; nothing was recorded under `viewer:<id>` and prepared reports were not screened against each other.
   *Fix (`lib/saha/aggregate-delivery.ts` `reportView`, `lib/saha/aggregate-reports-server.ts`, `app/aggregate-reports-panel.tsx`):* the simpler of the two safe options was taken: **cells are never returned to a person**, before or after sending. The read returns the manifest (definitions and versions, row count, SHA-256) and, for each block, the team, indicator, version, unit and whether it was withheld (cohort or differencing). The figures go to the destination only. Screening each preparation and approval as a release to `viewer:<id>` would have refused reports for people who had seen related indicators, and would still have left cells shown after sending to managers who never had them screened. An approver approves the parameters, manifest and hash; to see the figures, a person runs the same indicators on the indicator page, which screens them for that person (slices 6s, 6aa).
2. **Releases recorded only the window start (high, slices 6aa and 7g).** An aggregate period can end in the past. A report for 1–19 September and one for 1–20 September to the same destination had the same release identity: the second replaced the first in the ledger, both were accepted (6 visits, then 7), and one visit was revealed. The population check did not catch it either, because it counted every window until now (`t1.mjs`).
   *Fix (`lib/saha/indicator-differencing.ts`, `lib/saha/indicators.ts`, `lib/saha/aggregate-delivery.ts`):* a release for a period that ended before the report was computed records its end (`u`). The window end is part of the series-check window key, of the prune identity and of the population check's `same` test, so such a release never replaces, or is skipped by, a release for another end. The population check counts each windowed measure over `[start, end]` (a measure describing a moment, such as open follow-ups, at `end`). When the windows of a measure nest (each inside the next) the check splits each person's value into window-to-window increments as before; when they do not nest (two periods that overlap, or two moments), each person's whole profile across the shown cells is one atom, which is more conservative. Indicator page queries, and reports for a period ending today, still end now and record no `u`, so their behaviour is unchanged.
3. **Report payloads could fill the operations row (high, slice 7g).** Full payloads were stored in the operations row beside the 1 MB differencing ledger and never pruned. One 10-team, 8-indicator, village-grouped report was 287 KB (`t3.mjs`); a few such reports take the row past D1's 2 MB value limit, after which every operations write fails (aggregate reports, maintenance, incidents) and the ledger write throws, so indicators are refused organisation-wide.
   *Fix (`lib/saha/aggregate-delivery.ts`, `lib/saha/aggregate-execution.ts`), without a migration:* a payload is kept only while its report is open (prepared, approved, sending or outcome unknown) and is removed when the report closes (received, rejected, not sent or expired); a closed report keeps its parameters, manifest hash, row count, steps and receipt, and its payload can be recomputed from the pinned definitions. A payload over 150 kB (`PAYLOAD_BYTES`) is refused at preparation with advice to choose fewer teams or indicators or a coarser grouping, and preparation is refused when all stored reports would exceed 450 kB (`REPORTS_BYTES`). With the ledger's 1 MB bound that leaves at least 550 kB of the 2 MB value for the rest of the operations document, so neither reports nor the ledger can make an operations write fail.
4. **The report limit counted closed reports, and prepared reports never expired (medium, slice 7g).** After 200 preparations, including cancelled and never-approved ones, the organisation could never report again.
   *Fix (`lib/saha/aggregate-delivery.ts`, `lib/saha/aggregate-execution.ts`):* the limit (`REPORT_LIMIT`, now 20) counts open reports only. A report not approved within 24 hours of preparation, or approved and not sent within its 24-hour approval window, is closed as expired at the next preparation, with a step on the report and an `aggregateReportExpired` audit event; approving a report prepared more than 24 hours ago is refused. Sending after the approval window is still refused with the existing message.
6. **A late send result was discarded (low, slice 7g).** When a receipt lookup took over a send still in flight after 30 seconds, the original send, on finishing, returned without recording the destination's answer.
   *Fix (`lib/saha/aggregate-execution.ts`):* an answer that arrives after another request took over, or after the report closed, is appended to the report's steps and the audit chain as evidence ("Late answer to an earlier send/receipt lookup …"). It resolves the report only while its outcome is still unknown (sending or uncertain) and the answer is definitive (received or rejected); otherwise the state is unchanged and the step says so.
7. **Maintenance (low, slice 7g).** `lookupAggregateReport` was not in the maintenance allowlist although maintenance says receipt lookups stay available, and the send checkpoint did not re-check operating mode (the EHR publication does).
   *Fix (`lib/saha/operations.ts`, `lib/saha/aggregate-execution.ts`):* the lookup is allowed during maintenance; inside the checkpoint, on the fresh read, a send to an organisation paused for maintenance is closed as not sent ("the organisation was paused for maintenance"), the reserved attempt is released and nothing is sent.

Not fixed in this slice (time): finding 5 (ledger byte use: per-receiver ledger rows, dropping `c` and `m` for suppressed cells; the population screen's CPU cost for large organisations), finding 8 (refused indicator candidates are not recorded or rate-limited) and finding 9 (the correction task's 60-output cap has no administrator path, and its indicator-release count covers every receiver organisation-wide). They remain open and are listed in Limits.

## Limits

- Finding 5 is not fixed: one village-grouped query with eight indicators records about 70 kB at 150 villages and 185 kB at 400, so a viewer's 250 kB ledger bound can refuse a second such query for seven days and about five such queries fill the organisation's 1 MB bound; the population screen takes about 1.5 s of CPU for 3,200 people and two windows.
- Findings 8 and 9 are not fixed (see above).
- Closed reports are not archived to their own table: they stay in the operations row in compact form (about 1–3 kB each), so after several hundred reports the 450 kB budget refuses new preparations until archiving is built.
- A report's steps grow with each receipt lookup; repeated lookups on one report are not bounded beyond the 450 kB budget's headroom.
- When the windows of a measure do not nest, the whole-profile check is more conservative than the increment split and withholds more.
- Approvers do not see the figures they approve; they approve the parameters, manifest and hash (see finding 1).
- These fixes have not been independently reviewed. BR-INT-09, BR-REP-04 and BR-REP-05 keep their status; their current text and evidence are updated.
