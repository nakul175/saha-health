# Development slice 6v — in-app alert delivery and escalation

1 October 2026 UTC (2 October IST). Builds on slices 6g, 6h, 5e and 5h. Synthetic data only; formal acceptance unchanged.

## What changed

- **Delivery rules 1.0.0** (`lib/saha/alert-delivery.ts`, `lib/saha/alert-delivery-server.ts`). Alerts are still computed from what each person may already see (alert rules 1.2.0, unchanged). Saha now keeps delivery state for them:
  - an **occurrence** opens when an alert first appears in the view of a member who owns it, and closes when it is no longer present. Operations and owner alerts have one occurrence per organisation (their acknowledgement is shared); supervisor alerts have one per supervisor, because each sees counts for their own teams;
  - each owning member (the 6h `canAcknowledge` rule: owners and administrators for operations alerts; supervisors and non-administrator owners for supervisor alerts; owners for budget and invoice alerts) receives **one in-app notification per occurrence**, recorded with its delivery time and the count from that person's own view. An ASHA or clinician receives nothing;
  - an **acknowledgement** (6h) clears the occurrence's notifications for everyone holding them. When it stops applying (the count changes or 24 hours pass) they are shown again as unread;
  - an occurrence that stays open and unacknowledged past its threshold (critical 30 minutes, high 4 hours, normal 24 hours), or that re-opens after an acknowledgement made during it, **escalates once** to the next role on its ladder: operations → owners, supervisors → owners. Owner alerts (budgets and invoices) are already at the top. Escalation goes only to active owners of the same organisation, never to the supervisor whose scope raised it, and is recorded on the occurrence (time, target role, reason, number of recipients). The escalation notice names the alert, when it opened and, for supervisor alerts, whose care scope raised it; it carries no count from another person's scope.
- **No external messages.** Notifications are rows in Saha's own database shown in the app. Nothing is emailed, texted, pushed or sent outside Saha.
- **Storage** (migration `0014_alert_delivery`): `alert_occurrences` and `alert_notifications`, outside the organisation record. A delivery never moves the organisation or patient revision, so it cannot make a teammate's command conflict. Rows hold alert ids, titles, counts and member ids only — no patient names or clinical content. Both tables are in the data-exit export.
- **Idempotent.** Occurrences are unique per open alert and scope (partial unique index), notifications per occurrence, person and level, and the escalation update applies only while `escalated_at` is empty; writes use `INSERT OR IGNORE` or guarded updates. Repeated or concurrent evaluations never duplicate a delivery or an escalation. An evaluation reads the open occurrences and their notifications first and writes only what changed (new deliveries, clearings, re-openings, closings, escalations), so a steady state costs three small reads and no writes beyond computing each owner's view.
- **When it is evaluated.** The Worker has no background timer in this deployment, so evaluation happens when people use Saha: on each inbox read (`GET /api/alerts`) and Operations read, and after every organisation-wide command on the state that command already loaded. Patient-scoped commands (slice 0a) load only their patients, so they are picked up by the next read. A read evaluates at most once a minute per Worker instance unless the organisation or a patient record changed since the last evaluation. Evaluation is best effort: if it fails the read or command still succeeds and existing notifications are shown.
- **Inbox read.** `GET /api/alerts` returns the signed-in member's own notifications (active first, then those cleared in the last 7 days; at most 30), the unread count, the rules, and for owners the last 20 escalation records. Every notification is rechecked against the person's current membership on each read: a former supervisor no longer sees supervisor notifications, a non-owner no longer sees escalations, a suspended member is refused.
- **Mark read.** Command `alertInboxMarkRead` (1–50 notification ids) marks the viewer's own notifications read. It writes only those rows and a command receipt; it does not change the organisation record. Ids that are not the viewer's, or that their current membership may not hold, answer 404 as if absent. The same check runs again when an idempotent request is replayed. It remains available during maintenance.
- **Bell.** A bell with the unread count sits in the top bar on every workspace page for members who own any alert kind. It opens a list of deliveries and escalations with severity, title, count when delivered, delivery, read and clearing times, "Mark read", "Mark all read" and "Open Operations". It refreshes after each workspace change and every minute while the page is visible.

## Evidence

- 22 contract assertions (`tests/alert-delivery-contract.mjs`): ladders and thresholds; recipients by role and scope (administrators never receive clinical backlog alerts, ASHAs nothing); first evaluation opens and delivers; repeat evaluation opens nothing; closing; threshold boundaries for critical, high and normal; owner alerts never escalate; escalation recipients exclude the scope holder and suspended owners and carry no count; no second escalation; acknowledgement clears and stops escalation; re-open after an acknowledgement made during the occurrence escalates at once, but an older acknowledgement or another supervisor's does not; who may hold a notification; mark-read command shape; idempotent SQL, writes limited to changes, and no external delivery path.
- HTTP/D1 assertions in `tests/alert-delivery-smoke.mjs` (run inside `tests/api-smoke.mjs`): unauthenticated 401, stranger 403, worker inbox empty; a new critical incident delivers to the administrator and owner and not the supervisor, with no patient names; repeat reads add no rows; acknowledgement clears both; a second critical incident re-opens and escalates the same occurrence to owners only, once; mark-read refused for another person's notification, an unknown id and an extra field, then stored and replayed with the same key; resolving both incidents closes the occurrence and clears every notification.
- Existing suites updated: the data-exit contract now expects 31 exported tables and names migration `0014_alert_delivery`; the safe-logs contract lists the two new reviewed console lines (they log an error name only).

## Limits

- Detection depends on traffic. With nobody reading or changing anything, nothing is evaluated: an alert opened at night is delivered when someone next opens Saha, and a threshold escalation happens at the first read after the threshold. The five-minute detection target in NFR-18 is therefore not met. A scheduled trigger (a Cloudflare Cron Trigger calling the same evaluation every few minutes) would bound detection and escalation time independently of use; it is not configured here.
- In-app only. There is no email, SMS, push or paging channel, so a person who is not using Saha is not reached. Any external channel needs approved recipients, content review and consent before it is built.
- Thresholds and the ladder are engineering defaults, not approved response targets. Each occurrence escalates once (two-level ladders); there is no repeat reminder after escalation.
- For organisation-shared alerts, one owner's view showing the acknowledgement applying clears the occurrence for all; owners with differently scoped counts can see the alert as unacknowledged on Operations while their notification is cleared.
- The once-a-minute throttle is per Worker instance (in memory), so several instances may evaluate in the same minute; the unique keys keep this harmless.
- Notifications and occurrences are not pruned yet. Counts on a notification are the count when delivered; Operations shows the current count.
- Alerts still point to Operations tabs, not to individual tasks, provider calls or receipts.

## Correction — 1 October 2026 (slice 6z)

An organisation acknowledgement counted for an occurrence when any recipient's view showed it, although care-report alerts have a different count in each member's scope: one owner's acknowledgement cleared another owner's notification and stopped escalation. Escalation notifications were not tied to the guarded escalation update, so a concurrent evaluation could leave them on a closed, never-escalated occurrence, and restore was not guarded against a closed occurrence. Data exit exported every member's notifications. Slice 6z clears each recipient's notification only when their own view shows the acknowledgement and treats the occurrence as acknowledged only when every view does; inserts escalation notifications only when this evaluation's guarded update escalated the occurrence; guards restore; and exports only the exporter's own notifications. See [slice 6z](SLICE-6Z-SIXTH-REVIEW-FIXES.md), findings 2, 3 and 6.

## Update — 2 October 2026 (slice 10a)

Where a Cron Trigger is configured (the hosted staging Worker), a scheduled job now evaluates every organisation's alerts and in-app delivery about every five minutes through the same writer, so detection and threshold escalation no longer wait for someone to open Saha there, and it records the evaluation time in the organisation record. Delivery is still in-app only. The statements above about the Worker having no background timer describe every host without a trigger. See [slice 10a](SLICE-10A-SCHEDULED-TRIGGER.md).
