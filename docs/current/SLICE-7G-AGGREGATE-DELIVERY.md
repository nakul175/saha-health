# Development slice 7g — aggregate report delivery to aggregate-only destinations

1 October 2026 (UTC). Builds on the indicator registry and differencing protection (6s, 6w), reconciled exports (6k), material-read and external-request audit (6t), task trace (6y) and adapter operations (7e). Synthetic data only; formal acceptance unchanged. Four commands are added (`prepareAggregateReport`, `approveAggregateReport`, `sendAggregateReport`, `lookupAggregateReport`) and one route (`GET /api/aggregate-reports`); no patient command and no migration.

## What changed

- **A path to aggregate-only destinations.** Slice 7e let an owner mark a programme destination aggregate-only, but Saha had nothing to send it. An owner, or a supervisor for their own teams, can now prepare an aggregate report for such a destination from named indicator definitions (one to eight; the current version of each is pinned as `id@version`), a period of whole days ending no later than today (at most 366 days) and one or more teams in their own reporting scope, with an optional grouping (none, village, programme or age band). Owner authority does not widen the teams: an owner reports on their own teams, as for indicators. Administrators and ASHAs cannot prepare, approve or send. Code: `lib/saha/aggregate-delivery.ts` (pure parts) and `lib/saha/aggregate-execution.ts` (steps that read and write storage).
- **What the payload holds.** For each team, the indicator query of slices 6d and 6s runs filtered to that team, over the period. The payload (contract `saha-aggregate-v1`) carries only the cells the indicator page would show: counts from 1 to 4 and proportions built on them are suppressed, with one more cell where a total would reveal it; a team below the minimum cohort, a definition below its own minimum cohort and a definition withheld by differencing protection appear as withheld blocks with no cells. It carries a manifest of each definition and version with the SHA-256 of its canonical form, the number of cell rows and the SHA-256 of the canonical JSON (sorted keys) of the report, definitions and blocks, so a receiver can recompute it. No patient identifier, name, visit or record field is in it. A report in which every block is withheld is refused.
- **Differencing protection for the destination.** Before preparing, the report is screened against the destination's recent releases in the 6s/6w ledger (the receiver is `destination:<integration id>`, hashed with the organisation as before), so a remainder of 1 to 4 people, numerators or denominators across reports to the same destination is withheld. Sending records the release in the ledger as a release to that destination, on the ledger's own compare-and-swap statement, after the checkpoint and before the request. A person's own indicator queries and a destination's releases are separate receivers. The ledger code (`readLedger`, `writeLedger`, `candidateReleases`) moved unchanged from `indicators-server.ts` to `lib/saha/indicator-ledger.ts` so both paths write releases the same way.
- **Approval by a different person.** A report is prepared, then approved by a different authorised person (an owner or supervisor whose teams include all of the report's teams) naming the SHA-256 they reviewed; a different hash is refused (409). The approval lasts 24 hours. The preparer or the approver may send it.
- **Payload minimisation check (BR-INT-09).** Before anything is prepared, before the checkpoint and again inside it on a fresh read, the payload is walked: any patient-level field (for example `personId`, `visitId`, `name`, `abha`, `phone`, `facts`, `notes`), any field outside the aggregate contract, any string value equal to a patient id, visit id, name, ABHA number or address or external identifier in the organisation, any released count, numerator or denominator from 1 to 4 (or denominator minus numerator from 1 to 4 for a proportion), and any suppressed cell that carries a value are refused. Each run is recorded on the report (time, stage, passed, fields and cells checked, the first problems). A failure before the checkpoint closes the report as not sent with reason `minimisation`; inside the checkpoint it stops the request.
- **Sending follows the external-execution rules.** Before the checkpoint: the report must be approved and within its window, the destination must still be aggregate-only with the 7e `aggregate` operation permitted, the minimisation check must pass, and the report is recomputed from its recorded parameters (pinned versions, period, teams, grouping and preparation time) and screened against the destination's current releases: if the result differs from what was approved (records changed, or another report to this destination since approval makes part of it unsafe), the report is closed as not sent with reason `changed`. Then the request is recorded as an `externalRequest` access-audit event (6t; if it cannot be written nothing is sent and the report stays approved), the checkpoint is stored (`sending`, attempt 1), and on a fresh read the operation and payload are checked again and the release written. Any refusal there sends nothing, releases the attempt and closes the report with the refusal (operation, setting version, stage `checkpoint`, reason). The report id is the idempotency key. A response that does not match the receipt contract leaves the outcome unknown: the report is never sent again, only looked up (`lookupAggregateReport`, which needs the 7e lookup operation and makes a separate lookup request). Every step is stamped by `linkTrace` with its command, receipt key and audit event (6y). Nothing is retried automatically.
- **The demonstration destination.** Only demonstration (`demo`) destinations receive aggregate reports in this release; an HTTP destination is refused before preparing because no aggregate contract has been agreed with a partner. The in-process simulator returns a receipt `AGG-…` derived from the destination and report key; a destination with the "uncertain" behaviour (the community MIS simulator) stores the report but loses the first response, and a lookup then finds the receipt. In an environment where demonstration connectors are not allowed (slice 6n), preparing or sending to one is refused (this guard is not covered by a test in this slice).
- **History on the destination's screen.** An aggregate-only programme destination's card on the Connections page shows "Aggregate reports": a form to prepare a report (indicators from the current catalogue, teams in the viewer's scope, period and grouping), and the history of reports whose teams are all in the viewer's scope with state, period, teams, definitions, row count, SHA-256, receipt, attempts, who prepared and approved, the latest minimisation check, and, on request, the released cells, the manifest and every step with its linked command. It offers Approve (to someone other than the preparer), Send (to the preparer or approver) and Look up receipt (for an unknown outcome); the server decides. The 7e note that aggregate-only destinations receive nothing is replaced. `GET /api/aggregate-reports` serves this for owners and supervisors; the operations read leaves the reports out, as it leaves out the ledger.
- **Audit.** `prepareAggregateReport` (definitions, rows, SHA-256, check result), `approveAggregateReport`, `externalRequest`, `sendAggregateReport` or `lookupAggregateReport` (checkpoint), `aggregateReceipt`, and `aggregateReportRefused` for a report closed as not sent.

## Limits

- Demonstration destinations only. No live destination, aggregate contract or partner has been configured or tested; the receipt contract mirrors the programme outbox's but is Saha's own.
- A report is recomputed over current records; nothing snapshots the records behind a sent report, so a later report for the same period can differ. Current-state measures (for example open follow-ups) reflect records at preparation, not at the period end.
- Differencing protection compares one destination's releases over seven days, as for a person's queries (slice 6s/6w limits apply: no collusion across destinations or viewers, no non-nested overlaps). Reports sent more than seven days apart are not compared, which is weak for monthly reporting.
- Teams are reported separately; there is no total across selected teams, no reporting calendar or schedule, no cancellation command for a prepared or approved report (an approval simply expires after 24 hours), and a destination answer of "not found" leaves the outcome unknown for the destination to resolve.
- Reports are kept in the organisation's operations record (up to 200) and are not part of the exception queue or the patient trace route; their steps are traced on the report itself.
- BR-INT-09 and BR-REP-05 stay partial.
