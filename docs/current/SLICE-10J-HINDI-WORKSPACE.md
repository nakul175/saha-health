# Development slice 10j — Hindi for the ASHA journey in the workspace (draft translation)

2 October 2026 (IST). Business requirement BR-UX-05 asks for localised use; until now only the field notebook (slice 6f) had Hindi and the main workspace was English. This slice adds a small message catalogue to the workspace, a language choice saved for each member in the organisation, and a draft Hindi translation of the screens an ASHA works in. **The Hindi text is a draft. No qualified translator has reviewed it**, so every Hindi screen says so, BR-UX-05 stays partial and NFR-15 stays unverified. No server authorisation changed. Synthetic data only; formal acceptance unchanged (0 of 176).

## What changed

- **Message catalogue** (`lib/saha/messages.ts`, no dependency). Each entry is an `[English, Hindi]` pair keyed by screen (`nav.`, `work.`, `patient.`, `capture.`, `review.`, `receipts.`, `clinical.` plus shared `common.` and `role.`). English is the default and the fallback: a missing or blank Hindi string shows the English text, and an unknown key shows the key, never a blank label. Keys are typed (`MessageKey`), so a misspelt key in a screen fails the type check. One/other plurals use `<key>.one` and `<key>.other`. 402 keys.
- **Language choice** (`setMyLanguage`, `lib/saha/platform.ts`). A member chooses English or Hindi for themselves in the organisation: self only (a `userId` or any other field is refused with 422), `version`-checked (409 when stale, 422 when the language is already the chosen one), audited (`setMyLanguage`, naming that Hindi is a draft translation), at most 2,000 records per organisation, and stored as `operations.memberLanguages[userId]` in the organisation's existing operations record (no migration). The language follows the member in this organisation only. It changes no identity, role, team or access and does not touch the membership version. Like the display name (slice 9b) it is not on the maintenance allow-list, so it is refused (423) while the organisation is paused; no test exercises that case. The snapshot carries the signed-in member's own `language` and `languageVersion`; the members list carries no one's language.
- **Screens** (`app/i18n.tsx`, `app/workspace.tsx` and the panels). `useI18n()` gives `t`, plural, label and sign-off helpers; outside a provider everything is English. The sidebar has a Hindi/English choice (buttons with `aria-pressed`, each named in its own language and tagged `lang`). Translated in Hindi: the navigation and top bar, the environment strip and notices, My work (statistics, visit list, filters, rail), the patient page (banner, safety notices, previous visits), visit capture (including source and time per measurement and other units / unusual values), Review & act, Receipts, the visit rail and conflict dialog, the clinical review panel and its safety-case dialog, the Permissions and receipt dialogs.
- **`lang` attributes.** Translated content is inside `lang="hi"` (the sidebar parts, the main inset, and the dialog and select portals, which do not inherit it). Regions not translated yet are wrapped as `lang="en"` islands so a screen reader switches voice. The sidebar language names, the Field notebook link and the notice carry their own `lang`.
- **Notice on every Hindi screen.** A strip under the environment banner (`role="note"`) says in Hindi, then English, that the Hindi text is a draft translation not reviewed by a qualified translator, that names, values, notes and protocol text are shown as recorded, and, where it applies, that some panels on the screen are still in English (or the whole screen, for views not translated).
- **Never translated.** Data values (names, villages, programme names, sex, notes, receipt messages, identifiers), clinical rule text from protocols and services, record contents, measurement units (mmHg, kg, °C, UTC, weeks, days, years) and numbers; digits stay Western; dates keep the `en-IN` format. The urgent-finding tag is bilingual ("तत्काल समीक्षा · Urgent review") so the danger sign is never Hindi-only.
- **Still English (marked `lang="en"`, noted in the strip).** Programme evidence questions, the action plan and record tools (protocol content or clinician and owner tools); sign-off, entry and fact verification, unusual-value confirmation, trace, assistant, media and referral request panels; the household, identity, rights and ABHA dialogs opened from the record tools; and every supervisor, owner and administration view (Care handover, Outreach, Clinical governance, Pilot readiness, Expansion, Operations, Programmes, AI tools, Connections, Activity log, Organisation & team).

## Decisions

- **Units stay Latin** in the workspace (mmHg, kg, °C, weeks, days), unlike the notebook, which writes किग्रा, सप्ताह and दिन. The brief says units and numbers do not change; a reviewer may choose to localise them later, which is a catalogue edit.
- **Island rather than half-translation.** A panel with protocol or record text mixed into its sentences is left whole in English and marked so, instead of Hindi labels around English clauses.
- **No migration.** The choice is a small versioned map in the operations record, as the display name is, so no schema change and no restore-harness change (`lib/saha/store.ts` is untouched; the choice is read in `server.ts`).

## Limits

- Draft machine-quality Hindi written by the builder agent. No qualified translator, clinician or ASHA has read it; clinical wording ("तत्काल समीक्षा", "सब ठीक नहीं", referral text) in particular needs review before any real use. Not independently reviewed.
- Coverage is the ASHA journey only; see "Still English". A Hindi session therefore moves between Hindi and English panels on one page, which the strip says.
- Server messages (refusals, receipts, audit text) and the hidden text inside the mobile sidebar sheet are English; toast messages show Hindi text without a `lang` tag. Browser date and number controls follow the browser's locale.
- Two focus stops on the Hindi capture screen show no visible focus indicator under the audit's check (the tab panel around the form and the observation date and time field). They may exist in English too; this slice did not compare or fix them.
- The automated checks find a subset of accessibility failures. No screen-reader run, no user testing with ASHAs, no Devanagari font review on real devices.
- Requirement counts unchanged: 14 implemented-local, 151 partial, 2 not-built and 9 unverified; BR-UX-05 stays partial and NFR-15 unverified.
