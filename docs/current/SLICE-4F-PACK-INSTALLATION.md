# Development slice 4f — governed installation of a programme pack

2 October 2026 (IST). Part of slice 4 of the development-completion plan (NEXT-DELIVERY row 4: pack migration and installation). Synthetic data only; formal acceptance unchanged. **Installed packs are not clinically approved content.** The review step here is a governance check of a manifest's structure, conflicts and the reviewer's authority; it is not clinical approval, and nothing in this slice supplies institution-approved pack content.

## What changed

- **The need.** BR-PROG-08 asks for new approved programme packs through a governed runtime installation process. Until now the catalogue was the three packs compiled into the Worker (maternal v1 and v2, TB v1, hypertension v1), so a new pack or a new version needed a code change and a deployment.
- **Manifest.** An owner uploads a pack manifest as JSON (format `saha-pack-manifest-1`) in the command body, at most 8,000 characters (the command body limit stays 20,000 bytes). The schema is strict: unknown keys are refused anywhere. A manifest holds a pack id (lower-case slug), a version, a name, one of the existing programme categories, a purpose, 1–25 questions, an optional follow-up interval (`followUpWithinDays`, 1–365, with a short note), up to ten escalation rules, the earlier versions it declares a reviewed mapping from (`migratesFrom`) and a change note. A question is either a measurement of a shared visit fact (`weight`, `systolic`, `diastolic`, `temperature`, `weeks`, `coughDays`; the question id is the fact key and the unit is fixed: kg, mmHg, °C, weeks, days) or a choice with 2–8 options; it may be shown only when an earlier choice question has a given value, and may name the id it was renamed from. Escalation rules use the governed protocol rule vocabulary (a question, `gte`/`lte`/`eq`/`missing`, a value, urgency `review` or `urgent_review`). There is no code, no expression language and no free-form condition: every field is a label, an enumerated value, a number or an id that must resolve inside the manifest.
- **Structural checks** beyond the schema: unique question and rule ids; a measurement uses its fact key and canonical unit; a choice has no unit and no repeated options; a show-when condition names an earlier choice question and one of its options; a rename needs a declared mapping and cannot keep the old id; a rule names a question the pack asks, compares a measurement with a number, a choice only with `eq` and one of its options, and `missing` takes no value; `migratesFrom` names only earlier versions. The first six problems are returned in a 422.
- **Conflicts** with the compiled packs and the other installations (rejected and withdrawn ones excepted), refused with a 409: the same pack id and version already present; a version not higher than the pack's highest; a new pack id that does not start at v1 or declares a mapping; a new version that moves a pack to another programme category; a new pack named like an existing one; a mapping from a version that is not compiled or activated; a rename from a question no mapped version asks; and a question id that another pack defines differently (label, unit, fact, options or condition) — a visit captures each question id once across its packs, so shared questions must match. The conflict check runs again at activation against the catalogue as it is then.
- **Lifecycle** (`operations.installedPacks`, at most 12 per organisation): `packInstallStage` (owner) validates and stages a draft with the manifest's SHA-256 hash; `packInstallReview` (a clinician who is not the uploader — the slice 5b/5d independent-review rule) approves or rejects the exact staged manifest hash, and may review an approved version again; `packInstallActivate` (owner) activates an approved version after rechecking that the reviewer is still an active clinician with the same membership version and that nothing now conflicts; `packInstallRetire` (owner) retires an active version or withdraws a staged or approved one. Each step needs a reason of at least 10 characters, appends `{state, at, by, reason}` to the installation's own history and writes one audit event naming the pack, version, installation and manifest hash and stating that it is not clinical approval. Installations are never deleted. Role checks run on the server and again when a command is replayed by its key; the reviewer-is-not-uploader check is part of that replay check.
- **Catalogue.** `lib/saha/programmes.ts` now resolves packs from the compiled list plus the organisation's installed versions: activated versions can open episodes and be migration targets; retired installed versions stay resolvable for the episodes pinned to them but open nothing and receive no migrations. Pack ids are slugs; `enrolProgramme` and `programmeMigrationApply` accept any slug and refuse a pack outside the organisation's catalogue (`Choose an available programme pack.`). New visits (including field-prepared and field-registration visits) copy the installed pack definition into the visit like a compiled one, so a confirmed visit keeps the definition it was captured under even after retirement.
- **Adoption.** A new installed pack opens episodes on its first activated version. Activating a new version of an existing pack does not change the version new episodes open on: as in slice 4e, an owner's reviewed migration adopts it. Retiring the adopted version moves adoption back to the highest activated version below it (or, for an installed pack with nothing left, closes it to new episodes); the audit event says which version new episodes now open on and how many open episodes stay pinned to the retired one.
- **Migration to an installed version.** The slice 4e dry run and apply work unchanged against installed versions: the target is the latest activated version, `migratesFrom` and `renamedFrom` in the manifest declare the reviewed mapping, and episodes on versions without a declared mapping are listed for manual action (`no-reviewed-mapping`).
- **Read.** `GET /api/pack-installations` (owners and clinicians; ASHA, supervisor, administrator and non-members refused; no query parameters) returns the notice that installed packs are not clinically approved, the manifest format and limits, and each installation newest first: pack definition, schedule and escalation text, the escalation rules, the changes from each mapped earlier version (the 4e `packChanges`), current conflicts for those not yet active, the number of open episodes on the version (a count), its history with staff display names, and whether the reader may review, activate or retire it. No patient data. `GET /api/programme-migration` lists installed and retired versions in its catalogue. The workspace snapshot carries the pack catalogue (id, version, name, whether it can open episodes, whether it is installed) so the enrolment form offers activated installed packs, marked "installed pack, not clinically approved".
- **Screen.** Organisation & team gains a "Programme pack installation" panel next to "Programme pack versions": the not-clinically-approved notice, the manifest box (paste, read a `.json` file, or insert a synthetic example), staging with a reason, and each installation with its questions, schedule, escalation, mapping changes, conflicts and history, with Approve/Reject for an eligible reviewer, Activate for an owner, and Retire/Withdraw.

## Limits

- **Not clinical approval.** No institution-approved pack content exists; the reviewer checks structure, conflicts and the declared mapping, not clinical correctness. Packs used in tests are synthetic.
- The schedule interval and escalation rules are validated, stored and shown; nothing schedules follow-ups or escalates from them automatically, and they are not wired into the governed protocol checks (which keep their own reviewed rules).
- Programme categories are the existing five; a pack cannot add a category, so MIS and EHR routing, reports and indicators that key on the programme category are unchanged and do not distinguish installed packs within a category.
- No translated content, no per-team adoption, no answer migration between versions, and no import/export of installations between organisations. Installations are kept in the organisation's operations record (bounded at 12 manifests of at most 8,000 characters) and travel with it in the data exit and restore paths like the rest of that record; they are not published across organisations.
- The static Programmes catalogue page still lists the compiled packs only; installed packs are listed in Organisation & team and offered in the enrolment form.
- A retired version cannot be reactivated; install a higher version instead. An approved version whose reviewer lost authority needs a fresh review before activation.
- BR-PROG-08 stays partial: this is a local, synthetic governed path, not a production publication process with clinical sign-off. BR-OPS-03 and NFR-19 (unified publication and rollback across packs, languages, knowledge, models and mappings) are not materially changed beyond pack versions and keep their text.
