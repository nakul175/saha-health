## Resulting product behavior

Live `/api/operations` clinical JSON and CSV require the current reporting role or effective reporting delegation, normal current cohort and reporting purpose. Owners retain the same patient-scope constraints. Both formats use one protected projection; no frozen edition, accepted provider body, clinical fact, confirmation or old receipt is rewritten.

A cohort/contributing group smaller than five, a small joint contribution profile, a recoverable subtraction from recent live releases, untracked legacy disclosure history or exhausted protection capacity withholds the entire patient-derived projection. Numeric values are `null` in JSON and `Withheld` in CSV, with a reason. Programme/destination collections are unavailable (empty arrays accompanied by `privacy.withheld`, not evidence of zero records); the phone UI explicitly says those details are withheld. Daily dates/window metadata remain, with null counts. CSV control totals reconcile the protected unavailable state, not source counts. The export retains user, role, scope, UTC window, export identifier, checksum/row count and the draft label; report definition is 1.2.0.

A safe sufficiently large cohort remains usable. Exact per-person event/contribution counts are calculated using the unchanged `careReport` semantics and reconciled to its totals. A hundred events from one person remain one contributor. Current backlog, daily confirmations, programme/destination details and agent activity are included in the protection profile. Per-person inputs are partitioned once so calculating contributions does not repeatedly scan the entire organisation. No clinical denominator is inferred. Distinct active workers are always explicitly withheld: the existing full audit count does not provide certified per-patient additive actor attribution. This is an honest remaining measure limitation, not a patient denominator or inferred zero.

Returned report-derived alerts use the protected projection. Withheld/denied operations reads do not trigger clinical in-app alert evaluation; they retain existing notifications and normal nonclinical displayed alerts. A released safe cohort retains ordinary evaluation. The separate `/api/alerts` and other command/scheduled evaluation paths are not repaired by this slice: the actual adverse driver retains an authorised one-person `referrals-undated` inbox count as an open privacy counterexample. These paths need a separate governed software follow-on; this document does not claim complete aggregation protection across every product channel.

## Shared comparison contract

The existing per-viewer indicator records, calculation/suppression/differencing gates and bounded CAS ledger remain. New human live operational and indicator releases additionally record an organisation-comparable bridge in the same private `indicatorReleases` value. The bridge uses a purpose-specific organisation-domain person hash, fixed aggregate contribution count pairs and salted series/scope hashes. It stores no raw person IDs, names, locations, clinical values, clinical notes or reasons. The bridge never expands a viewer's authorised records: each candidate is constructed from that viewer's current normal/reporting cohort.

The bridge splits the union of retained hashed contributors by previous and proposed presence/count profiles. Every new or split atom must be zero or contain at least five contributors. This is deliberately conservative and may withhold more than the actual aggregate equations require. It compares cross-viewer, cross-surface, overlapping/non-nested cohorts, scope withdrawals, multiple primitive/paired/joint-zero profiles, changing windows and contribution drift. A person withdrawn from current scope is not silently lost from the private earlier comparison basis. Matching counts and safe same-cohort repeated views remain usable.

Changed counts produce distinct retained profile identities. Repeated identical profiles may replace their identical copy, preserving the counts; no current protecting count is discarded to make space. Matching original indicator release digests are retained with their individual source times and expire by the same seven-day time window. Legacy releases without exact bridge evidence remain untracked/unknown and withhold a new live disclosure. Malformed profile/version/domain/scope/source history fails closed. This does not retrospectively certify old releases or revoke downloaded copies.

All private bridge rows and source binding bytes count toward existing bounds: 168 hours, 400 releases/250,000 bytes per receiver and 4,000 releases/1,000,000 bytes per organisation. Reaching capacity withholds operational counts and retains current evidence; indicator queries retain their existing refusal status. There is no additional identity store, migration, approval workflow, background retry or public telemetry payload. The existing export omission of `indicatorReleases` also omits the private bridge. Cross-destination/legacy aggregate delivery is not silently upgraded; missing exact comparable history remains conservative.

## Current authority, audit and failure behavior

The projection is screened/reserved before disclosure and audited before return. After the final read/export audit, current trusted membership/role/owner status, reporting purpose, reporting reach/delegations, exact authorised patient/visit versions and recomputed report basis are checked again at the original cutoff. Source or authority drift refuses with 403/409, without returning stale report bytes. Safe alert evaluation occurs after a first fresh guard and is followed by another fresh guard. Existing CSV `recordExportAudit` freshness/delegation evidence is preserved.

Ledger storage or audit failure refuses without raw counts. JSON protection/audit moves no organisation/patient revision and creates no clinical command receipt. Existing CSV audit/operational receipt behavior is retained. Read recovery does not rerun a clinical mutation, create a clinical key, contact a provider or automatically retry a dispatch. No new identity/authority comes from public input.
