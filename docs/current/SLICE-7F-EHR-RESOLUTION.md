# Development slice 7f — definitive resolution of uncertain EHR publications

2 October 2026 IST (1 October UTC). Builds on the EHR publication checkpoints (`lib/saha/ehr-execution.ts`, [EHR-ABDM.md](../../EHR-ABDM.md)), slice 6t (external requests recorded before they are sent), slice 6u (publications closed as not sent), slice 6y (`linkTrace`), slice 7d (replay soak, which found that about one in five EHR publications stay unresolved under high fault rates because the adapter has no definitive not-found) and slice 7e (adapter operation permissions). Synthetic data only; formal acceptance unchanged. One owner command is added (`setEhrLookupPolicy`); no route, no patient command and no migration.

## What changed

- **A declared lookup policy per EHR connection.** Each EHR connection can carry a receipt-lookup policy in its existing JSON (`ehr.lookup`): whether the partner contract makes the identifier search authoritative, the partner's stated consistency delay in seconds (0 to 86,400), the named reconciliation owner, who set it, when, the reason, and up to 20 earlier settings. Code: `lib/saha/ehr.ts` (`setEhrLookupPolicy`, `lookupPolicyOf`). The search itself is unchanged: `GET Bundle?identifier=urn:saha:documents|<publication id>&_count=2`, the stable identifier Saha already sends with conditional create.
- **Default off: earlier behaviour unchanged.** A connection with no policy (version 0) is not authoritative: only exactly one matching document equal to the published one resolves a publication, and an empty or multiple result leaves it unknown, word for word as before. The policy has its own version and does not change the connection version, so publications already uncertain stay lookup-able after it is set.
- **Owners only, with a named owner.** `setEhrLookupPolicy` names the connection, the policy version the owner reviewed (0 for none), the flag, the delay, the reconciliation owner (or none) and a reason, such as the partner contract reference. Only an organisation owner may send it (checked on the server and in the replay block's owner-only list). Declaring the search authoritative requires document storage to be enabled (which already requires identifier search in the capability test) and a named reconciliation owner; the owner must be an active organisation owner or administrator, the people who see delivery exceptions (422 otherwise). A stale version is refused (409), as is a change that changes nothing. Each change writes a `setEhrLookupPolicy` audit event. Reconfiguring capabilities keeps the policy; with document storage turned off it is shown and kept but inactive.
- **Authoritative search outcomes** (`classifySearch` in `lib/saha/ehr-provider.ts`). Matches are search entries with mode `match` (an `OperationOutcome` entry is not a match); the count is the larger of the entries and `total`. Exactly one match equal to the published document: accepted, receipt recorded, as before. No match after the attempt window (45 seconds from the publication checkpoint) plus the partner's consistency delay: `not-stored`. No match inside that time: still unknown, with the time after which a lookup can be definitive. More than one match, or one match that is not the published document: `conflict`. A result whose `total` disagrees with its entries is treated as no answer (unknown). Each definitive answer is recorded on the publication as `resolution` (outcome, matches, policy version, time, lookup key, detail).
- **Not stored is never resent.** A `not-stored` publication is closed: approving it again returns its state without a request, and lookups are refused. Its visit is no longer held, so a care worker may prepare a new publication through the normal review and approval path; that publication has a new identifier. Saha never prepares or sends one by itself.
- **Conflicts go to the reconciliation owner.** A `conflict` publication holds its visit (no new publication may be prepared or sent for that visit and link), is never resent or looked up again, and is listed in the exception queue (rules 1.3.0) as a high-severity row whose owner is the connection's named reconciliation owner and whose recovery is to compare the matching documents with the partner; Saha does not choose between them. Rows for publications whose outcome is still unknown also name the reconciliation owner when one is set. A `not-stored` publication is listed as a normal-severity row for the patient's care workers until a later publication of the same visit to the same link exists. Conflicts count in the "EHR publications without a final receipt" alert and in the data-exit handover list.
- **Each lookup is a governed external request.** Before preparing, the lookup checks the 7e lookup operation (unchanged) and the attempt limit: at most 24 lookups per publication (`EHR_LOOKUP_LIMIT`, counted at each lookup checkpoint), after which the lookup is refused and the reconciliation owner resolves it with the partner. Inside the checkpoint, after the 7e recheck, the lookup writes the slice 6t `externalRequest` audit event (identifier, lookup number, policy) before the request; if that event cannot be written, nothing is requested and the publication stays unknown. The lookup's trace step `lookup request` carries a `linkTrace`-style reference to that audit event, and the final step is stamped by `linkTrace` as before. A lookup never sends a document (asserted for every lookup in the soak).
- **The attempt window is enforced, not assumed.** A not-stored answer relies on the publication's write having been made (or abandoned) within 45 seconds of its checkpoint. The checkpoint now records `publishStartedAt`, and the write request (15-second timeout) must start within 30 seconds of it; a publication whose patient check and authority recheck take longer is closed as not sent before the write. This applies to every connection; at normal timings nothing changes. A definitive answer also stands only if the connection's lookup policy is still the one the lookup was made under; if an owner changed it during the lookup, the publication stays unknown. A definitive state written by a concurrent lookup is never overwritten.
- **Screens.** The EHR connection card shows the lookup policy and reconciliation owner to everyone; owners get a "Receipt lookup policy" disclosure (authoritative flag, consistency delay, owner picker limited to owners and administrators, reason). A publication shows its lookup result (one matching document, not stored or conflict), its policy version and the number of lookups. The trace colours `not-stored` and `conflict` as needing attention. Checkboxes inside connection cards now keep their own size: the existing `.integration-actions button{width:100%}` rule had stretched every one of them (the EHR read and storage boxes and the 7e permitted operations too), wrapping their labels one character per line.

## Decisions

- The policy is per connection and is a declaration of the partner contract by an owner; Saha cannot verify it. It defaults off so nothing changes until an owner states it with a reason.
- One matching document that is not the published document is treated as a conflict under an authoritative policy (the partner holds something under Saha's identifier that is not what Saha sent), and stays unknown under the default policy as before.
- The consistency delay is counted from the end of a fixed 45-second attempt window after the publication checkpoint (the same bound the lookup already used for a stuck checkpoint), not from the time Saha recorded the unknown outcome, because a lost write could reach the partner at any time inside that window. The 30-second write deadline makes the window a property of the code.
- The lookup limit is 24 per publication: generous enough that no soak publication reached it, low enough to stop an unbounded loop of requests to a partner. A lookup inside the delay counts toward it.
- The reconciliation owner must be an owner or administrator so that they can see the redacted delivery rows. Resolving a conflict inside Saha (recording which partner document stands) is not built.

## Limits

- The mock partner's search is consistent and no request arrives late. A real partner that stores a document after its stated delay would make a `not-stored` answer wrong, and a new publication would then create a second document for the visit; this is why the flag is an owner's declaration of the partner contract, defaulting off. Partner acceptance evidence for authoritative search and conditional-create deduplication is still required.
- A conflict stays in the exception queue until the partner relationship is resolved outside Saha; there is no command to record the reconciliation outcome, close the conflict or accept one of the documents.
- The lookup limit is per publication, not a time-based budget, and is not raised as an alert of its own. Publications already uncertain before this slice have no recorded checkpoint time; their delay is counted from their last recorded state time, which is later than the actual attempt and so only delays a definitive answer.
- Persistence in the soak and contract tests is an in-memory compare-and-swap model; the policy command is also exercised through HTTP and D1 in the API suite, but no authoritative lookup runs against D1 or a live EHR. EHR amendments and destination version conflicts (BR-INT-21) are not built.
- A publication's outcome step keeps its checkpoint time, as before this slice, so the trace lists the later `lookup request` step after the outcome it led to.
- BR-INT-04 and BR-INT-21 stay partial.
