# Development slice 10b — out-of-app notification channel (demonstration simulator)

2 October 2026 (UTC). Operational alerts (slice 6g to 6v) reached people only inside Saha: the top-bar bell shows them to the members who own an alert and escalates an unacknowledged one once, to owners, also inside Saha. A person who is not signed in saw nothing. This slice adds an owner-managed notification channel: a recipient list with tiers, an interval after which an unacknowledged alert goes to the next tier, a message log with delivery receipts and audit for every message, and an owner screen. The only adapter is a demonstration simulator: it never contacts an address or a number, and the server refuses to run it outside the synthetic and training environments. Live delivery to real people needs approved recipients, contact details, consent and message review, none of which exist; they are not claimed here.

## What changed

- **Recipient lists** (command `setNotificationChannel`, owner only, version-checked, audited; stored in the `operations` JSON as `notificationChannel` with its own version, the last 10 earlier settings kept as a summary). Up to 30 recipients, each an active member of the organisation with a tier (1 to 3), a channel (SMS or email) and the alert kinds they receive. A member may be listed only for kinds their current role holds, the same rule as slice 6v: operations alerts for owners and administrators, supervisor alerts for supervisors and owners, budget and invoice alerts for owners. A role that changes later is checked again each time a message is planned, so a demoted member stops receiving. The owner sets the escalation interval (5 to 1440 minutes, default 30). Replaying a saved request with the same idempotency key returns the first result; a change that alters nothing is refused with 409 "Nothing changed". Recipients carry no contact details: the command schema is strict and refuses a phone number, an address or any other field.
- **Demonstration simulator** (`lib/saha/notification-channel.ts`, mode demo). Allowed only when the environment policy of slice 6n permits demonstration connectors (`synthetic` and `training`); staging and production, and an unrecognised environment, are refused with 409 when the setting is saved and no message is planned or sent on any later evaluation. It does not call `fetch`, does not read a secret and has no network code; the contract test scans the source for that. A message is stored with a demonstration label in place of an address (`demo-sms-xxxxxxxx (not a phone number)` or `demo-xxxxxxxx@simulator.invalid`, derived from the recipient) and a simulated receipt `{simulated: true, contacted: false, provider: 'saha-demonstration-simulator', status: 'simulated-delivered'}` with a receipt id.
- **Messages carry no patient data.** A message is built from an allowlist: alert title, severity, a count, a reason (first notice or escalation) and the path link `/?view=operations`, in an SMS of at most 160 characters or an email with a subject. Alert titles are fixed text from the alert rules and carry no free text. No patient name, identifier, visit, phone number or free-text note enters a payload (the safe-logs rules of slice 6l). The contract test composes every one of the 14 alert kinds in both channels and checks the shape, the length and that no seeded patient string appears. A count is only ever the recipient's own view of the alert (a supervisor's alert counts that supervisor's care scope); where the recipient has no view of it the message says the count is not shown. In the owner's message log, the count of a care-scoped alert is shown only on messages addressed to the owner reading it and withheld from the others.
- **Escalation and evaluation** (`planNotifications`, a pure function: configuration, environment, open occurrences, the views of the alerts, the keys already sent, the members and a time in; messages out; no storage and no clock). The ladder of an occurrence has one rung per tier that has at least one eligible recipient; a tier with no eligible recipient is skipped, so the next tier is not delayed. The first rung is due when the occurrence opened and each further rung one more interval later, counted from the alert's `opened_at` of slice 6v. A rung is sent only while the occurrence is open and its recipients' views do not all show it acknowledged. An evaluation that runs late sends every due rung and records both the time it was due and the time it was sent. A closed or acknowledged occurrence plans nothing more; an alert whose acknowledgement stops applying (slice 6v) carries on from where it stood, and an alert that closed and opened again is a new occurrence with its own messages.
- **Where it runs.** `evaluateChannel` is called at the end of `evaluateDelivery` (slice 6v), which runs when alerts or Operations are read, after organisation-wide commands and from any scheduled trigger that calls `evaluateDelivery`, so the evaluation is shared and a scheduled trigger inherits the channel without further code. On reads the existing once-a-minute rule of `evaluateIfDue` applies (unless the organisation or a patient record changed); after an organisation-wide command it runs every time. A failure in the channel is recorded in the evaluation result and never fails the read or the command.
- **Idempotence and audit.** One message per alert occurrence, tier and recipient: a unique index `(org_id, occurrence_id, tier, user_id)` on the new table `notification_messages` (migration 0015) makes a repeated or concurrent evaluation harmless. Each message row and its audit event (`notificationSent`, actor `system:notification-simulator`) commit together in one access-audit style commit (slice 6t, now allowed to write the `notification_messages` side table through `ACCESS_SIDE_TABLES` in `lib/saha/audit-chain.ts`), so there is no message without an audit event and no audit event without a message; neither moves the organisation or patient revision. The audit event is in the verified hash chain. At most 40 messages are sent per evaluation, in commits of 20; the rest follow on the next evaluation.
- **Owner screen** (Organisation page, `app/notification-channel-panel.tsx`, owners only; `GET /api/notification-channel`, owner only, measured as route `notification-channel`). Setting: on or off, the interval, each eligible member with tier, channel and kinds, a reason. Open alerts with their tier schedule (scheduled, due, partly sent, sent, not needed after an acknowledgement). The message log, latest 100 with the total, each message showing the demonstration address, "Show what was sent" with the exact subject and body, the simulated receipt, an escalation tag and, when a message went out two minutes or more after it was due, how late. The screen states that nothing is sent to any address or number. A deep link `/?view=operations` opens the Operations page.
- **Exit and export.** `notification_messages` is a table of the data-exit export of slice 6m (tables 31 to 32, schema marker `0015_notification_channel`).

Existing suites that touch the changed shared code (access audit 35, audit chain 48, data exit 39, alert delivery 23, alerts 25, environment 26, safe logs 12 with the same nine reviewed console calls) pass; the data-exit contract's expected table count moved from 31 to 32 with the new table. ## Limits

- Demonstration only. A real channel needs approved recipients, contact details held under the privacy notice and consent, a reviewed message text, a provider contract and a delivery receipt from the provider; the simulator's "receipt" is Saha's own record that it stored the message. Turning on a live provider would be a separate slice.
- The five-minute detection target of NFR-18 is still not met: the evaluation runs when someone uses Saha (reads, organisation-wide commands) or when a scheduled trigger calls `evaluateDelivery`. Until a trigger exists in the deployed Worker, a message goes out late when nobody is using Saha, and the log shows how late.
- Escalation counts from the occurrence's opening time, not from the time tier 1 was sent; with a late first evaluation a later tier can be due at once.
- At most 40 messages per evaluation; very large recipient lists drain over several evaluations.
- The environment cannot be switched during the HTTP smoke; refusal outside synthetic and training is proved by the contract test and by the browser check against Workers started as staging and production (local, not a deployed site).
- The link is a path (`/?view=operations`), not an absolute address, because no public base address is configured; a real channel needs a configured base address.
- Counts in messages are the recipient's own view; the owner log withholds the count of a care-scoped alert from messages addressed to someone else.
- A listed member who changes role or leaves stays on the saved list and receives nothing; the form lists eligible members only, so the next save drops them.
- No approved escalation ladder, detection target, on-call rota or quiet hours exist; the interval is an owner's setting. Built in a parallel round and not independently reviewed.
