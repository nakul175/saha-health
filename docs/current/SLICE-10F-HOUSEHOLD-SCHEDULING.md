# Development slice 10f — household visit scheduling and cross-pack referral consolidation

2 October 2026 (UTC). Built from GitHub `main` `150b9fc` (lane f of the ten-lane evening build; slices through 6ae and 9a–9c). Builds on slice 1 (households), slice 4b (action plan), slice 4d (referral sources) and slice 4f (pack installation). Synthetic data only; formal acceptance unchanged. No AI model is involved and nothing is sent: no message, no facility contact, no reminder. One certified patient command is added (`createProgrammeReferrals`, 53 in all) and two organisation commands (`householdVisitSchedule`, `householdVisitCancel`). No route (the household read gains a field) and no migration: slots are stored in the household's existing JSON document and referral needs in the referral's.

The spec rule this addresses is BR-PROG-05: scheduling "joins compatible tasks into a visit plan while preserving programme due dates and completion conditions"; one visit "completes eligible shared tasks, leaves incomplete activities open and does not duplicate a referral merely because two packs identify it"; two tasks with incompatible timing or privacy constraints "must not be silently treated as one".

## What changed

- **Household visit plan.** `GET /api/household?personId=…` now also returns `visitPlan` for a person in a household. It lists, for each current member inside the viewer's care scope, the open programme follow-ups (several programme episodes' follow-ups with the same title, due date and owner stay one line with every programme named, as in the action plan), the open referrals and the routine home visit, each with its own due date, attention (overdue, due today, upcoming, waiting …), owner and whether a planned visit already covers it. Members outside the viewer's scope are counted (`outsideScope`) and never named, itemised or described. A member whose record was merged is shown as merged with no items. The plan is computed on read from each member's own records with the action-plan functions; nothing is copied.
- **Household visit slots.** `householdVisitSchedule` (household, household version, a date from today to 90 days ahead, a window of morning, afternoon, evening or any, and for each covered member the follow-ups, referrals or routine visit it covers) records one planned slot; `householdVisitCancel` (household, version, visit, a closed reason) cancels it. Rules:
  - A slot is a planning record. It changes no due date, owner or completion condition and closes nothing: each covered item stays open until its own record is resolved (a follow-up resolved, a referral event recorded). The view shows, for every covered member, how many covered items are still open.
  - **Timing is never silently merged.** An item due before the slot date is shown as late by that many days (computed on read from the item's current due date, so a referral delay changes it). One item can sit in only one planned slot; a second request for it is refused until the first is cancelled.
  - **Each member keeps their own scope and facts.** The scheduling worker needs normal care access (team, and assignment for an ASHA; emergency access does not count) to every member covered and a care role in the household's team. A member who is not a current member and a member outside the worker's scope are refused with the same message, so the answer does not reveal membership. A slot holds only references (member, item kind, item id), a date, a window and a closed cancellation reason: no free text, so nothing about one member can be written where another member's worker reads it. Each covered member gets one audit line on their own record that names only that member's items; no line names another member. Observations are still recorded on each member's own visit.
  - **Visibility.** A worker sees a slot only for covered members inside their scope, and a count of covered members outside it. A worker who can see none of the covered members does not see the slot.
  - **Cancellation** is by the worker who planned the slot or a supervisor or owner of the household's team (supervision delegates included, checked through the slice 1e helper). Cancelled slots are kept and shown with the reason. A household holds at most 100 slots, 10 planned at a time, 30 members per slot and 20 items per member.
  - Slots use the household's version like other household commands, so a membership edit made while a slot is being planned is refused for refresh. Both commands are authorised again when an idempotent request is replayed.
- **Cross-pack referral consolidation.** `createProgrammeReferrals` (a confirmed, current visit whose programme capture lists the asking packs, and one to 12 requests of programme episode, need, facility and reason) groups the requests by need. Two requests are the same need when the need code (or, for "other", its named need), the facility (case and spacing ignored), the person and the visit all match.
  - The same need becomes **one** referral. It links every asking programme episode as a source (slice 4d `programme-episode` links, reusing a link already made by hand) and records, in `packRequests`, which pack and version asked, for which episode, with that pack's own reason and who recorded it. The referral's own visit remains its first source.
  - A later request from another pack for the same need and facility **joins the open referral** (its version rises, an audit line says who asked) instead of creating a second one. A repeated request changes nothing; a command whose requests were all already recorded is refused (409).
  - A different need, or the same need at another facility, is a **separate referral**. A closed referral (completed, declined, cancelled) is never reopened: a new request starts a new referral. Referrals created before this slice (`createReferral`, directory referrals) carry no recorded need and are never merged.
  - The need catalogue is local: antenatal review, TB testing, blood pressure review, nutrition review, medicine access, danger-sign review and a named "other". Packs do not declare needs; the worker records which episodes raised which need.
  - Guards match `createReferral`: a care role with normal access to the patient, a confirmed and current visit, a record that is not merged and identity that is not provisional. The command is a certified patient command (it names one visit and writes only that patient's referrals and audit).
  - The action plan names the asking pack on each of the referral's sources ("Asked by Maternal care v1"); referral cards show the need and which programmes asked.
- **Workspace.** A "Household visit plan" dialog beside "Household & proxies" in the patient page's record tools lists each member's items with tick boxes, plans a visit (date and time of day), lists planned and past visits with what is still open and cancels one with a reason. The "Prepare a referral" dialog gains a "Programme referral" form: tick the programme episodes that raised a need, name the need and facility, and one referral is recorded for them.

## Limits

- The late flag, the one-slot-per-item rule, the 90-day horizon and the need catalogue are local defaults, not an approved timing or privacy policy. Programme packs do not declare which needs they raise or which tasks may share a visit; the worker chooses, and the server only prevents duplicates and silent merging.
- A slot does not move due dates, send reminders, create visits or complete anything. Starting each member's visit and resolving each item remain separate steps on that member's record. A slot cannot be moved: cancel it and plan another.
- Plans exist for people in a household; a person's own plan is the action plan (slice 4b). Items other than programme follow-ups, referrals and the routine visit (safety cases, protocol reviews, outreach) are not offered for household visits.
- A worker who sees one covered member learns that the household visit exists and its date, window and the planner's name, which may be inferred to concern others in the household; the slot carries nothing more about the members they cannot see.
- Consolidation matches on need code, facility text and visit. A facility named differently ("District Hospital" and "DH") is a different facility; directory facilities are not matched by identifier. Two packs that should share a referral but were recorded for different visits are two referrals. Referrals made before this slice cannot be merged.
- Slots and referral needs are in the household and referral documents, not in new tables. A data exit includes the household row with its slots; the existing patient-scope rule leaves out the cover entries of members outside the exporter's scope. Slots and needs are not in the data-exit hand-over list, the indicator definitions or the MIS payloads. Household commands use the organisation-wide commit guard.
- BR-PROG-05 and BR-CARE-05 stay partial.
